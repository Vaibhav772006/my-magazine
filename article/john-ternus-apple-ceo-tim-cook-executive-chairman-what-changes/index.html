John Ternus Is Apple's New CEO: What Changes After Tim Cook and What to Watch

John Ternus replaced Tim Cook as Apple CEO on September 1, 2026. Here is who he is, what Cook does now, the challenges ahead, and the Nvidia twist.

The Empire Insight Official Cover Feature
The Empire Insight Editorial Board November 13, 2026 • Spotlight: John Ternus (CEO, Apple) • Business & Leadership
Leadership Spotlight 2026

After fifteen years, the most famous handover in corporate America has happened. On September 1, 2026, John Ternus, the hardware engineer who helped create the iPad, AirPods and Apple Watch, became CEO of Apple. Tim Cook, who took over from Steve Jobs in 2011 and turned Apple into a multi-trillion-dollar giant, moved to the role of executive chairman. Weeks later, Cook's name surfaced again when President Trump suggested him as the next CEO of Nvidia. Here is a complete, plain-English guide to the Ternus era: who he is, what changes at Apple, what stays the same, and what to watch next.

The Handover: What Happened and When

Apple announced the transition in April 2026, after unanimous approval from its board. Cook said at the time that it had been the greatest privilege of his life to lead the company, and described Ternus as a visionary and the right person to lead Apple into the future. Ternus joined the board of directors when he became CEO, and Cook stayed on as executive chairman.

The timing was deliberate. Cook remained CEO through the summer while working with Ternus on the handover, and on the company's July earnings call Cook called his successor one of a kind and said the transition would go seamlessly. The long runway was designed to avoid the kind of market shock that surprise CEO changes can cause. A Fast Company report on the transition noted that Apple tested and tightly controlled how the news would be released, because leadership changes can send stocks tumbling.

Ternus's first major public moments as CEO were expected to come quickly, including Apple's September 9 hardware event, where the company was widely expected to unveil new iPhones and possibly its first foldable device.

“ The hardest part of any succession is not choosing the next leader. It is letting the old one step back without letting the company wobble. — The Empire Insight Editorial Board

Who Is John Ternus?

Ternus joined Apple in 2001 and spent his career in hardware engineering, eventually becoming senior vice president of hardware engineering. In that role he oversaw the development of many of Apple's best-known products, including the iPad, AirPods, Apple Watch and the Vision Pro headset. He also became the familiar face of product launches, presenting Apple's hardware at major events such as WWDC.

He worked under Steve Jobs and has described Cook as his mentor. In his statement after the announcement, he said that having spent almost his entire career at Apple, he felt lucky to have worked under Jobs and to have had Cook as a mentor. Commentators often note that his rise had been anticipated for years. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman had reported that Ternus was the obvious successor, citing his technical depth, his rising public profile and the fact that he is roughly the same age Cook was when he took the top job.

His appointment marks the return of a chief executive with a product and design background, something Apple's last long-serving product-minded chief, Steve Jobs, also had. Cook came from operations and supply chain, which is why his tenure is remembered for efficiency, scale and services growth. Ternus comes from the engineering side, which suggests a different emphasis.

The Cook Era by the Numbers

To understand what Ternus inherits, look at what Cook built. According to coverage of the handover, Cook took the baton from Jobs in 2011 when Apple was worth roughly $350 billion and led it to a market value of around $4.5 trillion. Apple's shares rose more than 2,200 percent under his leadership, and annual sales grew from $108 billion to about $416 billion in the most recent fiscal year, according to one report.

Cook's success was built on a few pillars: an enormously efficient global supply chain, a growing services business including the App Store, iCloud and Apple Music, and a steady expansion of the product line from iPhone to Watch, AirPods and Vision Pro. The challenge for Ternus is that this machine is already enormous, so growth must come from new categories and new technologies rather than from simply doing more of the same.

What Tim Cook Does Now as Executive Chairman

Cook did not leave Apple. As executive chairman, he is expected to help with selected company matters, and Apple specifically said the role includes engaging with policymakers around the world. That is a meaningful detail, because Apple's business depends heavily on government relationships, from trade and tariffs to privacy rules and app store regulation. Analysts noted that keeping Cook in that role reduces the likelihood of an abrupt break in Apple's government, supply-chain and institutional relationships.

The pay structure was also disclosed. According to one report, Cook will receive a $2 million salary as executive chairman, with a target equity award of $45 million to be granted in fiscal 2027, half of it tied to Apple's stock performance against other S&P 500 companies.

Why Hardware Matters for the AI Era

The choice of a hardware leader is widely read as a strategic signal. Babak Hafezi, a professor of international business at American University, told Al Jazeera that Ternus's appointment is a subtle way of doubling down on hardware innovation as a core strategy, and that it does not refute the Cook era but marks a new stage that interweaves AI with hardware instead of treating AI as a standalone product. Aleksandar Tomic of Boston College argued that focusing on core products could help Apple lead the AI boom over the long run.

That idea fits Apple's long-standing approach. While rivals race to build the biggest models, Apple has tended to focus on devices, custom chips and the user experience, with AI features built into products people already own. If AI becomes a feature inside every phone, laptop and wearable, a company that designs both the silicon and the device has a natural advantage. If instead AI value concentrates in the largest cloud models, Apple could face pressure to partner or catch up.

“ Apple does not need to win the AI race by building the biggest brain. It needs to put the best one in your pocket. — The Empire Insight Editorial Board

The Challenges Waiting for Ternus

The new CEO steps in while Apple faces pressure on both the product and operational fronts, as one profile of the new CEO put it. Several challenges stand out.

  • Artificial intelligence. Apple is expected to keep proving that its approach to AI can compete with fast-moving rivals.
  • New product categories. A foldable device and further work on the Vision Pro line are among the areas observers are watching for the next growth chapter.
  • Regulation. App store rules, competition cases and privacy laws in the United States, Europe and Asia continue to shape Apple's services profits.
  • Supply chain and trade. Tariffs and geopolitical tension affect where and how Apple makes its products.
  • Succession depth. Apple has reshuffled its senior ranks, including reported moves such as Johny Srouji taking a chief hardware officer role, and a new leadership bench must prove itself.

The Cook-to-Nvidia Twist

The Apple story took an unexpected turn in November. After Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned, President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social that Cook should replace him. Neither Cook nor Apple immediately commented, and there has been no confirmation that Nvidia's board is considering him. Nvidia has appointed CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and said it is searching for a permanent successor, both inside and outside the company.

It is best to treat this as a suggestion and not a plan. Cook is Apple's executive chairman, a role Apple created to keep him engaged with policymakers, and moving to lead a company in a different part of the technology industry would be an enormous step. Still, the suggestion shows how highly Cook's reputation as a steady operator is rated, even by those outside Apple.

Lessons From Apple's Succession for Other Companies

Whatever happens next, the way Apple managed the handover offers a template that other boards can study.

  • Start early. Reports said Cook's successor was widely expected for years, and Ternus was given increasing public visibility long before the announcement.
  • Use a long runway. Months between the announcement and the effective date gave customers, suppliers and investors time to adjust.
  • Keep the former CEO useful. A defined executive chairman role with clear responsibilities, in this case policy engagement, avoids both a power vacuum and a power struggle.
  • Choose strategy, not just a person. The pick of a hardware leader says something about where the company wants to go.
  • Control the message. Careful communication reduces the risk of a market panic.

Apple Compared With a Sudden Exit

It is hard to ignore the contrast with Nvidia's abrupt change at the top. Apple's transition was announced months ahead, rehearsed in public, and supported by a named chairman role. Nvidia's was announced the same day it took effect, after days of reports. Investors tend to reward predictability, and the difference illustrates why governance experts push boards to treat succession as a standing agenda item and not an emergency response.

What to Watch Under Ternus

  • Product launches. Reception of the fall iPhone lineup and any foldable device, and signals about future categories.
  • AI strategy. How Apple weaves AI into its devices and whether it announces new partnerships.
  • Earnings and guidance. Whether growth and margins hold through the first full quarters of the new era.
  • Leadership changes. Further moves on the executive team and any new design or hardware appointments.
  • Regulatory outcomes. Rulings that affect App Store revenue and competition in key markets.
  • Cook's role. Any official update on his duties and any developments tied to the Nvidia suggestion.

The Bigger Picture

Apple's handover is a reminder that great companies are judged on how they renew themselves, not just on how they perform under one leader. The Cook years proved that a supply-chain mastermind could grow a design-led company beyond anyone's expectations. The Ternus years will test whether an engineer can steer the same company through the shift to AI, new device types and tougher regulation.

Product strategy under a new CEO takes time to show up, because Apple's major devices and chips are planned years in advance. That means the first products of the Ternus era reflect decisions made under Cook, and the true imprint of the new leadership will appear gradually. Patience is as important for observers as it is for the company.
