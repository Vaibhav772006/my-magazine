UPI Security: 7 Digital Payment Mistakes Every Indian Should Avoid

From fake collect requests to remote-access apps: seven UPI mistakes that drain bank accounts, and what to do within minutes of a scam.

The Empire Insight Official Cover Feature
The Empire Insight Editorial Board November 20, 2026 • Spotlight: UPI Security (Digital Payments Safety, India) • Technology & Security
Pay Safe

UPI makes paying a tea-stall owner as easy as paying a landlord. It also makes it easy for fraudsters to move money before you realise what happened. Government figures reported for FY26 up to November counted about ₹805 crore lost in UPI fraud across roughly 10.64 lakh incidents. Most of these cases didn't involve clever hacking. They involved ordinary people making one rushed decision. Here are seven digital payment mistakes every Indian should avoid, and what to do if you slip.

Mistake 1: Entering Your UPI PIN to Receive Money

This is the oldest trick, and it still works. A “buyer” on a classified site says they'll pay you, then sends a request. You see a PIN screen and think you're confirming the payment. In reality you're approving money leaving your account. Rule: receiving money never needs your PIN. If a screen asks for it, you're paying.

Mistake 2: Sharing Your PIN or OTP With Anyone

Your UPI PIN, card PIN, OTPs and CVV are for you alone. Bank staff, delivery agents, “KYC officers” and courier companies won't ask for them. If someone does, end the call. You won't offend a real bank by hanging up and calling the number on your card.

“You never need your UPI PIN to receive money. Anyone who says you do is stealing from you.— The Empire Insight Editorial Board

Mistake 3: Scanning Unknown QR Codes

A QR code that pays you doesn't exist. QR codes are for paying. Fraudsters stick fake QR codes over genuine ones at shops and parking meters or message them to sellers, claiming “scan to get your refund.” Always confirm the payee name shown in your app before you tap pay, especially at small shops.

Mistake 4: Trusting Customer-Care Numbers From Search Results

Scammers buy ads and plant fake numbers on map listings and forums. You search for your bank's helpline, call the first result and end up with a fraudster who happily “fixes” your problem by draining your account. Use the number printed on your card, in the bank's official app or on its verified website.

Mistake 5: Installing Remote-Access or Screen-Sharing Apps

If a caller tells you to install a screen-sharing app to fix a refund, a SIM issue or a failed payment, hang up. Once the app is active, the scammer can see your screen, your OTPs and your banking apps. No genuine bank requires this.

Mistake 6: Clicking Links or Installing APK Files From Messages

Messages with “traffic challan,” “electricity bill disconnection” or “KYC expired” often carry a link or an APK file. Installing it can give attackers access to your SMS and notifications, which is enough to intercept OTPs. Download apps only from the official Play Store or App Store, and don't open files forwarded in WhatsApp groups. The same caution applies to festival and sale season, when fake discount links multiply. See our Black Friday 2026 guide for how to spot fake offers.

Mistake 7: Waiting Too Long to Report a Fraud

Speed matters. If you suspect fraud, act immediately.

  1. Call your bank and ask to block the account or UPI access.
  2. Dial the national cyber crime helpline 1930.
  3. File a complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in.
  4. Save screenshots, transaction IDs, phone numbers and chat logs.
  5. Report the payment inside your UPI app as well.

The earlier you report, the better the chances that banks can freeze the money before it moves further.

What Is Being Done: New Safeguards

Regulators are tightening the screws. Reports say the RBI's revised authentication framework for digital payments took effect on April 1, 2026, requiring at least one dynamic security factor, and the NPCI has used measures such as device binding, transaction limits and AI-based fraud monitoring. Another set of RBI rules on compensation, complaint handling and bank response times is reported to take effect on January 1, 2027. These steps help, but you remain the final line of defence. Quick Safety Checklist

  • Set a screen lock and a separate app lock on your UPI app.
  • Use a different UPI PIN from your ATM PIN.
  • Keep daily UPI limits modest if you don't need high ones.
  • Turn on SMS and app alerts for every transaction.
  • Update your phone and apps regularly.
  • Be cautious of urgent messages, whether from a “relative,” a “bank” or a “police officer.” Hang up and call back on a number you trust.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need my UPI PIN to receive money?

No. You only enter your PIN to send money or authorise a payment. A screen asking for your PIN means money is leaving your account.

What should I do if I lose money in a UPI scam?

Call your bank immediately, dial 1930, file a report at cybercrime.gov.in and keep all evidence.

Are QR codes safe?

Scanning a QR code is for paying, not receiving. Always check the payee name before confirming.

Can scammers access my UPI through a fake link?

A malicious link or APK can install software that reads your messages and notifications. Avoid links and files from unknown senders.

Are there new UPI security rules in 2026?

Reports say the RBI introduced a revised authentication framework from April 1, 2026, and more rules on fraud compensation and complaint handling are expected from January 1, 2027.

Key Takeaways • UPI Security

Receiving money never needs your UPI PIN.

Never share PINs, OTPs or CVV, even with “bank staff.”

Don't scan unknown QR codes or install remote-access apps.

Verify customer-care numbers from official sources.

Report fraud fast: bank first, then 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in.
